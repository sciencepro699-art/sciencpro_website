Electromagnetic Induction in Daily Life

Move a magnet through a coil and make a current. Then change the voltage with a transformer and see why power stations send electricity at very high voltage.

1 · Magnet and coil

Drag the magnet in and out of the coil. Watch the galvanometer needle and find out for yourself when it moves.

Turns in the coil: 200
Magnet strength: 1×

2 · The transformer

AC goes into the primary coil. The voltage across the secondary depends on the number of turns. The power stays the same.

Primary turns Np: 1000
Secondary turns Ns: 50
Primary voltage Vp: 240 V
Power: 100 W

3 · Why transmit at high voltage?

A power station sends 100 kW through a line of 5 Ω. Raise the voltage with a step-up transformer and see how the heat loss in the wire changes.

Transmission voltage: 11 kV

Electromagnetic induction

Whenever the magnetic flux linked with a closed coil changes, an emf is induced and a current flows. No change, no current. More turns, a stronger magnet or faster motion give more emf.

change in flux → induced emf

AC and DC

DC flows in one direction only (cell, mobile phone). AC changes its direction again and again (home supply, mixie, grinder).

Cell → DC · Generator → AC

Generator

A generator changes mechanical energy into electric energy by electromagnetic induction. An AC generator uses slip rings. A DC generator uses a split ring commutator.

mechanical → electrical

Transformer

It works on mutual induction and changes AC voltage without changing the power. A step-up transformer has more turns in the secondary. A step-down has fewer.

Vs/Vp = Ns/Np · VpIp = VsIs

Power transmission

Raising the voltage lowers the current, and the heat loss I²R falls. Electricity is made at 11 kV, stepped up for the lines, stepped down near the town and reaches homes at 230 V.

11 kV ↑ 220 kV … ↓ 230 V

Household wiring

Wires: red is phase, black is neutral and green is earth. Appliances are connected in parallel. A switch is connected in series with the phase wire. The first parts are the fuse, main switch, ELCB and MCB board.

red phase · black neutral · green earth

Safety devices

A fuse wire (low melting point, in series) melts on too much current. An MCB trips and can be switched on again. An ELCB or RCCB cuts the supply on a current leak. An earth pin carries a fault current to the ground.

fuse: heating effect

Overload and short circuit

Too many or too powerful appliances on one line cause overloading. Touching phase and neutral wires causes a short circuit. Both give a huge current and can start a fire.

I = P ÷ V
