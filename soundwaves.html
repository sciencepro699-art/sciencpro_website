Frequency (f)

The number of complete oscillations (vibrations) a wave makes in one second. Higher frequency = higher pitch. Measured in Hertz (Hz).

f = 1 / T

Time period (T)

The time taken to complete one full oscillation of the wave. Frequency and time period are inverses of each other.

T = 1 / f

Wavelength (λ)

The distance between two consecutive compressions (or crests) of the wave. It shrinks as frequency rises, for a fixed wave speed.

λ = v / f

Amplitude (A)

The maximum height of the wave from its resting position. Higher amplitude = louder sound — it doesn't change the pitch at all.

Loudness ∝ Amplitude²
