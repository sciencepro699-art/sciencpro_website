Chemoreception in Organisms

Hormones are chemical messengers. Read the key ideas, then test yourself.

Hormones and target cells

Hormones are chemical messengers. A cell where a hormone acts is its target cell. It has receptors that fit the hormone. They join to form a hormone-receptor complex that controls the cell. Animal hormones travel in blood. Plant hormones move cell to cell or through xylem and phloem.

hormone + receptor → complex

Plant hormones

Auxin: stem growth, tropic movements, flowers and fruits. Gibberellin: breaks seed dormancy, fruit and stem growth. Cytokinin: cell division, delays ageing of leaves. Ethylene: ripening and ageing. Abscisic acid: dormancy and leaf and fruit fall.

ABA = dormancy

Synthetic hormones and flowering

Synthetic auxin (2,4-D) makes roots on cuttings and kills weeds. Synthetic gibberellin makes grapes bigger and sugarcane taller. Ethylene ripens bananas and tomatoes together. Flowering depends on day length (photoperiodism) and the pigment phytochrome.

photoperiodism

Thyroid and parathyroid

Thyroxine controls metabolism and body temperature. Too much causes hyperthyroidism, too little hypothyroidism, and lack of iodine causes goitre. Calcitonin lowers blood calcium. Parathormone raises it. Normal calcium is 9 to 11 mg/dL.

calcitonin ↓ · parathormone ↑

Glucose and diabetes

Fasting glucose is 70 to 100 mg/dL. Beta cells make insulin, which lowers glucose. Alpha cells make glucagon, which raises it. In diabetes mellitus glucose is high (FBS above 126). Type 1: beta cells destroyed. Type 2: lifestyle and poor insulin action.

insulin ↓ · glucagon ↑

Adrenal, pineal and thymus

Adrenal medulla: epinephrine and norepinephrine prepare the body for an emergency, together with the sympathetic system. Adrenal cortex: cortisol, aldosterone, androgens. Pineal: melatonin, the biological clock for sleep. Thymus: thymosin matures T lymphocytes.

melatonin = sleep

Hypothalamus and pituitary

The hypothalamus is the master controller. Its releasing hormones and inhibitory hormones reach the anterior pituitary through the portal vein and control tropic hormones (TSH, ACTH, GTH). ADH and oxytocin are made by the hypothalamus and released from the posterior pituitary.

hypothalamus → pituitary → glands

Growth disorders

Somatotropin from the anterior pituitary makes cells divide and grow. Low in childhood: dwarfism. High in childhood: gigantism. High after the growth phase: acromegaly, with big hands, feet and jaw.

GH: dwarfism · gigantism · acromegaly

Water balance

ADH (vasopressin) makes the kidneys take back more water. In summer sweating raises ADH, so urine becomes less. In winter ADH falls, so urine becomes more. This keeps the water level of the blood normal.

dry → more ADH → less urine

Reproduction and hunger

Testosterone: male organs, sperm, secondary sexual characters. Estrogen and progesterone: female organs, menstrual cycle, keeping the foetus. Prolactin: milk. Oxytocin: contraction of smooth muscle. Ghrelin makes you hungry. Leptin makes you feel full.

ghrelin: hungry · leptin: full

Pheromones

Pheromones are chemicals released into the surroundings to talk to the same species. Types: territorial, aggregation (food), alarm, trail (ants walk in a line) and sex pheromone (attracts mates). Farmers use pheromone traps to control pests.

pheromone trap = pest control
