CHAPTER 3 · BIOLOGY

Behind Sensations

Read the key ideas, then test yourself.

Stimulus and response

A stimulus is a change that causes a response. Receptors detect it and make an electrical signal (receptor potential). In the neuron it becomes an action potential, the nerve impulse. The brain then sends instructions to muscles and glands.

stimulus → receptor → brain → response

General and special senses

General senses (touch, pain, heat, pressure) have receptors all over the skin, muscles and organs. Special senses (vision, hearing, taste, smell) have receptors only in certain organs.

special senses: 4

Parts of the eye

Three layers: sclera (outer, with the cornea), choroid (middle) and retina (inner, with photoreceptors). The iris controls the pupil. The lens focuses light. Aqueous humour is in front of the lens and vitreous humour behind it.

cornea → pupil → lens → retina

Accommodation

The eye focuses on near and far objects by changing the curvature of the lens. For a distant object the ciliary muscles relax and the lens is thin. For a near object they contract and the lens becomes thicker.

near → thick lens

Rods, cones and colour

Rod cells (rhodopsin) work in dim light in black and white. Cone cells (photopsin) work in bright light and give colour. There are S, M and L cones for blue, green and red. Retinal comes from vitamin A.

rods: dim · cones: colour

Eye disorders

Short-sightedness (concave lens), long-sightedness, astigmatism (cylindrical lens), cataract (opaque lens), glaucoma (high pressure), night blindness (vitamin A deficiency) and conjunctivitis (infection).

cataract = opaque lens

The ear

Pinna → auditory canal → tympanum → ossicles (malleus, incus, stapes) → oval window → cochlea. The organ of Corti has the hair cells that make impulses. The auditory nerve carries them to the brain. The Eustachian tube balances pressure.

malleus · incus · stapes

Balance

The vestibular system of the inner ear keeps balance. Three semicircular canals sense rotation. The utricle and saccule sense straight-line movement. The brain also uses signals from the eyes and muscles.

semicircular canals

Smell, taste and skin

Smell: molecules dissolve in mucus and stimulate olfactory neurons. Taste: substances dissolve in saliva and stimulate chemoreceptors in taste buds (sweet, sour, salty, pungent, bitter, umami). The skin has receptors for touch, pain, heat and pressure.

chemoreceptors
