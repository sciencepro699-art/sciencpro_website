3 · Make an ester

An alcohol and a carboxylic acid react with conc. H₂SO₄ to give a sweet-smelling ester and water.

Substitution

One atom or group in a molecule is replaced by another. Methane and chlorine in sunlight give chloromethane and HCl.

CH₄ + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + HCl

Addition

Unsaturated compounds (with a double or triple bond) add atoms across the bond and become saturated. Hydrogenation uses a nickel catalyst.

CH₂=CH₂ + H₂ → CH₃–CH₃

Combustion and cracking

Burning in air gives carbon dioxide, water and heat. Thermal cracking breaks a large hydrocarbon into smaller ones on heating.

C₃H₈ → CH₄ + C₂H₄

Polymerisation

Many small monomers join to make a polymer. Addition polymers: polythene, PVC, Teflon, Orlon, natural rubber (from isoprene). Condensation polymers: nylon 66, bakelite, polyester.

n CH₂=CH₂ → (CH₂–CH₂)ₙ

Ethanol

Made by fermentation of molasses. The wash has 8–10% ethanol. Rectified spirit is 95.6%. Absolute alcohol is 100%. Power alcohol is a petrol–ethanol blend. Denatured spirit is made unfit to drink.

C₂H₅OH

Ethanoic acid

A carboxylic acid with the group –COOH. Vinegar is a 5–8% solution of ethanoic acid in water.

CH₃COOH

Esterification

Alcohol + carboxylic acid → ester + water, in the presence of conc. H₂SO₄. Esters smell fruity and are used in fragrances and flavours.

acid + alcohol → ester + H₂O

Soap

Soaps are sodium or potassium salts of fatty acids. Glycerol is the by-product of soap making.

by-product: glycerol

Medicines

Analgesics relieve pain, antipyretics lower fever, antiseptics kill germs on skin and antibiotics fight bacteria. Paracetamol and aspirin are examples.

paracetamol · aspirin
