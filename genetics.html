Genetics of Life

A gene, a chromosome, and DNA are often mixed up — but they're really just different zoom levels of the same thing. Step through them below, from a whole cell down to a single gene.

Pick a level to zoom in

Every one of your cells carries the same set of instructions, packed away in a very specific way. Tap through the steps to see how.

The DNA double helix

Two long strands twist around each other like a ladder. Each rung is a pair of chemical bases, and they only ever pair up one way.

Adenine (A) pairs only with Thymine (T)
Guanine (G) pairs only with Cytosine (C)

Each rung is one nucleotide pair. A nucleotide itself is made of a sugar, a phosphate, and one of these four bases — that's the basic building block the whole strand is made from.

Gene

Gene is a specific sequence of nucleotides in DNA. Proteins made from a gene's instructions produce characteristic features and control metabolic activities.

A sequence of nucleotides

Chromosome

DNA and histone proteins are a chromosome's main components. Numerous nucleosomes pack and coil together to form it.

Humans have 46 (23 pairs)

DNA

Deoxyribonucleic acid — the molecule that stores all of an organism's genetic instructions, shaped like a twisted ladder.

Shape: double helix

Base pairing rule

The four bases in DNA always pair the same way, which is what makes the double helix a stable, regular shape.

A–T and G–C

Nucleotide

The basic building block of DNA. Each nucleotide is made of a deoxyribose sugar, a phosphate group, and one nitrogen base.

Sugar + phosphate + base

Histone & nucleosome

Eight histone proteins join to form a histone octamer. DNA winds around it to form a nucleosome — the unit chromosomes are built from.

8 histones = 1 octamer
