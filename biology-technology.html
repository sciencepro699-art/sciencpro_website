CHAPTER 6 · BIOLOGY

Biology and Technology

Genetic engineering made simple. Read the key ideas, then test yourself.

Biotechnology and genetic engineering

Biotechnology uses living things or their parts to make products and services useful to humans. Genetic engineering creates desired traits by combining or deleting genes. The discovery of the structure of DNA speeded up biotechnology.

biotechnology = living things for human use

Recombinant DNA technology

It joins DNA pieces from two or more organisms. Restriction endonuclease is the genetic scissors that cut a gene. Ligase is the genetic glue that joins DNA pieces. A vector carries the gene. A host cell receives the recombinant DNA. Plasmids (circular DNA in bacteria) and some viruses are vectors.

cut → join → insert → express

Making human insulin

1. Cut the insulin gene from human DNA. 2. Cut the bacterial plasmid. 3. Join the gene to the plasmid with ligase. 4. Put the plasmid into a bacterium. 5. The bacteria multiply in a culture medium and make insulin. The next generations of bacteria also make insulin because the gene is copied.

human gene + plasmid → insulin

CRISPR

CRISPR edits DNA very precisely. It was designed from a natural defence of bacteria against viruses. Guide RNA (gRNA) finds the exact DNA segment. Cas9 enzyme is the scissors that cuts it. The bases are then edited to remove unwanted characters or add new ones.

gRNA finds · Cas9 cuts

Genetically modified organisms (GMOs)

Plants: Bt cotton (pest resistance), soya bean (weedicide resistance), golden rice (rich in nutrients), papaya (virus resistance). Animals: salmon fish (fast growth), Glo fish (fluorescence). Microbes: E. coli makes insulin, Pseudomonas putida breaks down waste, baker's yeast ferments, Rhizobium fixes nitrogen.

E. coli → insulin

Genome, gene mapping and HGP

Genome is all the genetic material of an organism. Gene mapping finds the location of genes in DNA. The Human Genome Project ran from 1990 to 2003. It found about 300 crore base pairs and 20,000 to 25,000 genes. Functional genes are only 1 to 2%. The rest is called junk DNA.

HGP: 1990 – 2003

Gene therapy

Gene therapy treats a disease by replacing a defective gene with a working one. In sickle cell anaemia: blood stem cells are collected, the healthy gene is put in using a virus as vector, and the stem cells are injected back. Somatic gene therapy affects only the patient. Germline therapy passes to the next generation and raises ethical questions.

somatic vs germline

DNA fingerprinting

99.9% of DNA is the same in all humans. The 0.1% that differs, mainly in repeated sequences of non-coding DNA, is unique to each person. Sir Alec Jeffreys discovered it in 1984. Uses: find culprits, find genetic disorders, prove parents and children, identify fossils, accident victims and endangered species.

unique pattern = identity

Organoids and 3D printing

Organoids are tiny 3D tissue models grown in the lab. They help study disease and test drugs. A 3D bioprinter uses scans (MRI or CT) and bioink (living cells and growth factors) to print organ models. This may solve the shortage of donors and organ rejection.

bioink + 3D printer

Microbiome

The human microbiome is all the microorganisms and their genes in the human body. They help immunity, digestion and making vitamins. The Human Microbiome Project began in 2007. It showed that disease can come from a change in the balance of microorganisms. Poop pills with good bacteria treat Clostridium difficile infection.

HMP: 2007

Artificial intelligence in biology

AI helps in disease diagnosis, drug discovery, personalised medicine, genome sequencing, gene editing, farming (soil and crop health) and pollution prediction. Bioinformatics analyses biological data with computer science, mathematics and statistics.

bioinformatics = biology + computing

Technology for the environment

Wildlife tracking sensors follow animal movement. Biodiversity data sensors use satellites, sound and camera traps. Cryopreservation stores cells and tissues at −196 °C. Bioremediation uses microbes and plants to remove pollutants. World Environment Day is 5 June and World Ocean Day is 8 June.

bioremediation = microbes clean up
