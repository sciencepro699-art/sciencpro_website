Properties of a gas

Three measurable properties: volume (the volume of the container), pressure (force on the walls from collisions) and temperature (in kelvin, K = °C + 273).

K = °C + 273

Boyle's law

At constant temperature, the volume of a fixed mass of gas is inversely proportional to its pressure. Double the pressure and the volume halves.

P₁V₁ = P₂V₂

Charles's law

At constant pressure, the volume of a fixed mass of gas is directly proportional to its temperature in kelvin.

V₁ ÷ T₁ = V₂ ÷ T₂

Avogadro's law

At the same temperature and pressure, equal volumes of all gases contain the same number of molecules. So the volume is proportional to the number of moles.

V ∝ n

The mole

One mole of any substance has 6.022 × 10²³ particles. Its mass in grams is its molar mass (the atomic or molecular mass).

1 mole = 6.022 × 10²³ particles

Moles, mass and molecules

To find the number of moles divide the mass by the molar mass. Multiply the moles by 6.022 × 10²³ for the number of molecules.

n = mass ÷ molar mass

Molar volume

At STP (273 K and 1 atm), one mole of any gas occupies 22.4 L.

1 mol gas at STP = 22.4 L

Ideal gas equation

Boyle's, Charles's and Avogadro's laws combine into one equation. R is the universal gas constant.

PV = nRT

Moles in equations

The coefficients in a balanced equation are moles. In N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃, 1 mol of nitrogen reacts with 3 mol of hydrogen to give 2 mol of ammonia.

N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃
