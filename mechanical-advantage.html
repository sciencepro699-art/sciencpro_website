Mechanical Advantage in Action

Balance a lever and see how its arms change the effort. Then try pulleys, a slope and a wheel and axle. A machine saves effort, but never work.

1 · The lever

Choose a type of lever. Set the load and the two arms. Then change the effort and watch the lever tilt. It balances when load × load arm = effort × effort arm.

Load L: 60 N
Load arm: 20 cm
Effort arm: 40 cm
Effort you apply E: 30 N

2 · More simple machines

Pick a machine. See how much effort it needs and how far you must move. The work is always the same.

Rope segments holding the load: 4
Load: 600 N
Load raised: 2 m

Length of the slope l: 8 m
Load: 600 N
Wheel radius R: 30 cm
Axle radius r: 5 cm
Load: 600 N

Mechanical advantage

How many times the load is the effort you would need without the machine. It is only a ratio, so it has no unit.

MA = Load ÷ Effort

Principle of a lever

A lever balances when the turning effects on both sides are equal. A longer effort arm needs less effort.

L × LA = E × EA

Three orders of levers

1st: fulcrum in the middle (scissors, seesaw). 2nd: load in the middle, so MA is always more than 1 (nutcracker, wheelbarrow). 3rd: effort in the middle, so MA is always less than 1 (tongs, forceps).

MA of a lever = EA ÷ LA

Pulleys

A fixed pulley only changes the direction (MA = 1). A movable pulley gives MA = 2. With n movable pulleys MA = 2ⁿ. In a block system MA = the number of rope segments holding the load.

n movable pulleys: MA = 2ⁿ

No gain in work

A machine lets you use less effort, but you must move through a longer distance. Work done by the effort equals work done on the load.

E × s = L × h

Inclined plane, wedge, screw

A longer, gentler slope needs less effort. A wedge is a double slope. In a screw, the length of one thread divided by the pitch gives the MA.

MA = length ÷ height

Wheel and axle

The effort is applied on the big wheel and the load is on the small axle. A bigger wheel gives a bigger MA.

MA = R ÷ r

Gears

Small toothed wheel driven by a big one gives more speed. For climbing a hill the engine is joined to a big toothed wheel: more turning force, less speed.

Big gear → force, small gear → speed
