Ore, mineral and gangue

A mineral is a natural compound of a metal. An ore is a mineral from which the metal can be extracted profitably. Gangue is the earthy impurity mixed with the ore.

ore + gangue

Concentration

Hydraulic washing (ore denser than gangue), magnetic separation (one is magnetic), froth flotation (sulphide ores) and leaching (ore dissolves in a solvent, e.g. bauxite).

4 methods

Calcination and roasting

Calcination: heating in limited air or no air. Carbonates and hydroxides give oxides. Roasting: heating below the melting point in excess air. Sulphides give oxides and SO₂.

2ZnS + 3O₂ → 2ZnO + 2SO₂

Reduction

Moderately reactive metals (Zn, Fe, Sn, Pb) are reduced from their oxides with coke or carbon monoxide. Highly reactive metals (K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al) need electrolysis. Mercury is obtained by controlled heating.

ZnO + C → Zn + CO

Refining

Liquation purifies low melting metals (Sn, Pb). Distillation purifies low boiling metals (Zn, Hg). In electrolytic refining the impure metal is the anode and pure metal the cathode (Cu, Zn).

anode: impure · cathode: pure

Flux and slag

A flux combines with the gangue to form molten slag. In the blast furnace limestone gives CaO, which joins silica to make calcium silicate slag.

CaO + SiO₂ → CaSiO₃

Iron and steel

The ore of iron is haematite (Fe₂O₃). Iron pyrite (FeS₂) is called fool's gold. Coke and carbon monoxide reduce the ore in the blast furnace. Steel is iron with a little carbon.

Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂

Alloy steels

Stainless steel (Fe, Cr, Ni, C) resists rust. Nichrome makes heating coils. Alnico makes permanent magnets. Alloys with the same components can have different properties because the amounts differ.

stainless · nichrome · alnico

Corrosion

Iron rusts with oxygen and moisture. A more reactive metal like magnesium or zinc in contact protects iron. Aluminium resists corrosion because of a thin oxide layer.

Mg protects Fe
